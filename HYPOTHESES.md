# Evidence status and hypotheses

Baseline: October 2–3, 2026 Work session. Read the original reports for citations and detailed limits.

## Observations

- The inspected SHAD0W and AetherForge paths express risk, resource, freshness, or lifecycle rules explicitly.
- TEMPER's result record reports specialist feasibility on one fixed intent task; external prediction artifacts were not recomputed.
- CipherLoop's inspected AST validator has limited local analysis semantics and a fixed confidence value of 0.9.
- TraceForge's inspected ingestion checks capture integrity rather than general task success. Its scripted baseline has two cases.
- Fracture's inspected core runtime raises `NotImplementedError`; topology comparisons remain proposals.
- Existing literature provides blackboards, state estimation, capabilities, formal interaction contracts, workflows, and runtime assurance. These are precedents, not a proof that their combination is universally better.

## Working hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Disconfirming result |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Consequential boundaries between intelligent components benefit from explicit semantic contracts, while internal intelligent computation may use whatever representation best preserves useful information. | Matched comparisons show no useful improvement in semantic errors, invariants, recovery, or substitution, or show representation loss and integration cost outweigh the gains. |
| H2 | Enforced authority can remain independent of proposer intelligence and representation. | Replacing a proposer requires bypasses or changes to permission semantics, prohibited effects occur, or useful legal completion collapses. |
| H3 | Recurring bounded semantic decisions can sometimes move to specialists with calibrated deferral at lower full-system cost and comparable risk. | Shift increases risk, coverage collapses, or lifecycle costs erase savings. |
| H4 | An explicit outcome/evidence path improves recoverability and auditability compared with accepting a producer's completion narrative. | Independent reconstruction cannot distinguish attempted from completed effects, misses consequential failures, or provides no advantage at matched cost. |
| H5 | Domain contracts can make components interchangeable despite heterogeneous internal cognition. | Replacement fails because consumers depend on private prompts, latent geometry, mutable memory, or undocumented semantics. |

## Counterevidence and qualifications

- ReAct supports language-mediated reasoning on particular tasks. Agent-BRACE represents beliefs with natural-language claims and ordinal uncertainty.
- Learned communication and ICML 2026 LatentMAS weaken an exclusively typed-symbolic prescription. Arbitrary cross-model latent interoperability remains unestablished.
- State abstraction can destroy important distinctions, optimality, or learning guarantees. Typed shape is not state sufficiency.
- Determinism does not make specifications correct. Verifiers can be incomplete, correlated with proposers, or based on wrong assumptions.
- Runtime assurance relies on suitable safety models and backup behavior. Proof and protocol guarantees are conditional.
- Durable histories do not guarantee exactly-once external effects. FLP limits unconditional deterministic consensus termination under its assumptions.
- Modularity introduces translation, orchestration, latency, and maintenance costs.
- No inspected evidence establishes the prevalence of unnecessary production LLM use.

## Unresolved questions

Semantic interface discovery and evolution; calibration under interaction and shift; correlated evidence fusion; authority tied to changing plans; ambiguous external effects; auditable latent communication; composition of statistical, formal, and real-time guarantees; full lifecycle economics.

## Experimentally established claims

**No proposed experiment in this research program was executed by these investigations.** No general architectural superiority claim is established.

External papers report task- or model-specific experiments under their stated conditions. TEMPER reports validation macro F1 0.9539 versus 0.8864 for its compared baselines, but this session did not reproduce it or establish calibration, OOS behavior, general-model superiority, or full-system savings. Repository tests and source inspection establish narrower implementation facts and should not be promoted into thesis-level results.
