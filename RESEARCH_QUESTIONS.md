# Durable research questions

Derived from both preserved investigations and the inspected repository artifacts. These questions are not claims that the leading architecture wins.

1. **What information must survive a boundary?** Can a maintainable state or belief representation preserve decision-relevant meaning under novelty, partial observability, compression, and schema evolution? When do prose or latent representations retain useful distinctions that bounded records lose?
2. **Who owns transitions and permission?** Can the proposer change while state ownership, authority, and accountability remain stable? How should delegation narrow, and how should permission be revalidated before effects?
3. **What independently establishes a result?** Which properties can be verified without trusting a producer's narrative? How do integrity, correctness, completeness, exploitability, and externally observed outcome differ?
4. **How should uncertainty change behavior?** What uncertainty should cross boundaries, how can evidence dependence be represented, and when is abstention, escalation, further observation, or reconciliation worthwhile?
5. **What coordination remains coherent under faults and heterogeneity?** Which combinations of workflows, actors, blackboards, probabilistic inference, planners, and control loops preserve invariants, recover useful work, and permit component replacement?

Cross-cutting economic question: does specialization or modularity reduce full-system loss and cost after extraction, verification, escalation, maintenance, and integration are included?

The most important unresolved question is whether useful semantic interfaces can be discovered and evolved cheaply enough without merely relocating intelligence and cost into adapters and assurance machinery.
